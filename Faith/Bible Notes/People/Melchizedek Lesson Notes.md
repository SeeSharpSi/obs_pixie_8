# The Function of the High Priest 
* Before Aaron, a priest was a tribal patriarch 
* Then, in Exodus 28:1, God sets apart Aaron and his sons as dedicated priests (with Aaron as Israel's first High Priest) 
* This starts the hereditary line of High Priests; also called the "Levitical Priesthood" because its lineage traces back to Levi 
* The most important role of a High Priest was to enter The Most Holy Place (which was kept behind a veil) to offer blood for the atonement of Israel's sins 
* Once a High Priest died (as all mortals did), another was selected from Aaron's bloodline 

handout: 
* Before Aaron, a priest was a tribal patriarch 
* Eventually, God made Aaron a dedicated High Priest (Exodus 28:1) 
* After this, priests had to be a descendent of Aaron, who was a descendent of Levi (hence the term "The Levitical Priesthood") 
* The most important role of a High Priest was to enter The Most Holy Place to offer blood for the atonement of Israel's sins, **including his own** 
	* The Most Holy Place was the spot in the temple that had a curtain in front of it. The curtain that tore when Jesus died 
* Priests were mortal, meaning their duty as priest would eventually end

# Author of Hebrews 
Argument of Omission? 
* The author of Hebrews was: 
	1. Maybe a hellenist - a Jew who used the greek Septuagint and was okay further integrating into the Roman culture 
	2. Very familiar with greek philosophy and literature 
* Back then, greek philosophers and writers were more prone to make arguments of omission (that is, recognizing that some thing _doesn't_ include another thing, and making an argument around that other thing). Our author, recognizing the importance of genealogies in the OT, realizes that Melchizedek doesn't have one and makes an argument from it 




# Typology 
Typology is a special kind of symbolism. In regards to scripture, we can define a type as a "prophetic symbol," and an antitype as the person/thing being foreshadowed 

Examples: 

| Type                                        | Antitype                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Noah's flood (Gen. 6-7)                     | Baptism (1 Pet. 3:20-21)       |
| The snake on the pole for healing (Num. 21) | Jesus' Crucifixion (John 3:14) |
| The passover lamb without defect (Ex 12:5)  | The Lamb of God (1 Pet. 1:19)  |
