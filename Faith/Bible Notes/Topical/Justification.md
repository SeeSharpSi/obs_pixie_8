## Introduction 
* People act as though the 1999 declaration between Lutherans and Catholics solved everything. I'm not persuaded of that. Some think it did nothing, and I'm not persuaded of that either. We got common ground on some things and important differences on other things 
* Even if we were together on justification the reformation wouldn't be over. Other differences still exist, and in some areas those differences have grown since the initial reformation 
* "I do think we have made some progress and a lot of the perceived differences result from misunderstandings" 
	* i.e. sometimes protestants miss the initial vs. final justification distinction 
	* the Roman Catholic position is _not_ the same legalism position as the Judaizers (wouldn't fall under the condemnation found in Galatians)
* We have a common core of agreement. This isn't a compromise 
	* Having patient dialogue and looking for where we have common ground where we're using terminology different is compromise 
	* There's a not insignificant core where we do agree on 
* If you do become too rigid on this you will not only reject contemporary Roman Catholics but many protestants throughout church history (e.g. Richard Baxter) 
* Some protestants say "if you don't have [imputation](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/essay/the-doctrine-of-imputation/) you don't have justification, which means you don't have the gospel" 
	* This would cause Augustine to not be a Christian (along with many others) 
* Catholics often misunderstand protestant views 
	* The caricatures of [Sola Fide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sola_fide) are just so bad 
	* It's almost as bad as the caricatures of [[Faith/Bible Notes/Topical/Sola Scriptura (Bible Notes)|Sola Scriptura]] 
* There's no problem with James 2 for a protestant 
	* Just because James says "we are justified by faith plus works" this is at odds with a protestant position 
* Eastern Orthodox Christians set [Theosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosis_(Eastern_Christian_theology)) against and over Sola Fide. This is a category confusion; there's no reason you can't affirm both things, they aren't mutually exclusive 
* We need to be careful here, because differences of phraseology and slogan and language are not always substantive differences. Just like agreements of language are not always substantive agreements 
	* We have to patiently dialogue with the other side 
	* Try to look at the other side at its best 
	* Inhabit their concerns. What are they worried about? 
	* These won't resolve all the problems, but we'll see we have overlap 

## Approaching Justification 
* This is the first introductory comment 
* One of the ways that we can try to understand another position is to see where they're coming from 
	* What keeps them up at night when they're worrying about this 
* If you try to understand what is animation Sola Fide try to interpret it in context 
	* Interpreting it in context means you see the angel of approach 
	* What are their concerns 
* For example, the way you approach something determines what you find 
	* If you're hungry, you're gunna relate to food a lot differently 
	* If you're lonely you relate to people a lot differently 
	* If you're tired you relate to sleep a lot differently 
* Similarly for a protestant, we approach justification with a recognition of the holiness of God and the desperation of our sinful status in that light 
	* Justification has to do with the legal dimension of our relationship with God and our salvation 
	* It refers to God's acceptance of us as righteous through Christ 
	* And it ultimately points ahead to our final acquittal on the day of judgement 
* You can see justification more clearly if you pit it against its alternative, its contrast, its opposite 
	* That is condemnation 
	* Think of the judge banging down the gavel and saying "righteous" 
	* That's a protestant understanding of justification 
* To understand where the protestant is coming from, people must understand the absolute of our desperate need in light of God's holiness 
	* A good exercise is to imagine the day of judgement, standing before God 
	* "In the shady cloisters of the schools anyone can easily and readily prattle about the value of works in justifying men. But when we come before the presence of God we must put away such amusements! For there we deal with a serious matter, and do  not engage in frivolous word battles. To this question, I insist, we must apply our mind if we would profitably inquire concerning true righteousness: How shall we reply to the Heavenly Judge when he calls us to account?" (John Calvin, _Institutes_, [3.12.1](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.v.xiii.html)) 
	* Even if you disagree with Calvin's language, it might help you understand the protestants mindset
