# Definitions 
* Sola Scriptura (Gavin Ortlund):
	* "The claim that the scriptures are the only infallible rule for Christian faith and practice"
	* There is a place for tradition. Creeds and councils can even be binding and authoritative. But tradition is fallible.

* Thoughtful Protestants have always recognized that tradition has an important place 
	* Both Luther and Calvin affirmed the early ecumenical creeds and councils 
	* Thoughtful Protestants recognize that there's oral tradition mentioned in the Bible 

* "There is no inspired table of contents / the church gave us the scripture"
	* The church didn't give us the Bible, it recognized the Bible 
	* The church no more gave us the Bible than Newton gave us gravity 
	* The church's role in respect to the canon is a thermometer, not a thermostat

## The (Rare) Word That Refutes Sola Scriptura 
* Sola Scriptura is that scripture is the only infallible rule of Christian faith 
	* There's other rules of faith, but they're always subordinate to scripture 
	* But what this _really_ means is that they're always subordinate to an individual's interpretation of scripture 
	* But _why_ would it be the only infallible rule of faith 
* The one word protestants overly rely on to support Sola Scriptura: **Theopneustos** 
	* 2 Timothy 3:16 - "all scripture is theopneustos. . ." 
* One argument for Sola Scriptura is that it is ontologically unique 
	* As far as we know, only scripture is theopneustos 
	* If something is theopneustos, then it comes directly from God 
	* If something is theopneustos, it _cannot_ be an infallible rule of faith for the Church 
	* It just means "God-breathed" is an assumption we should question
