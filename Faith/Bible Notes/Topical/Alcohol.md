# Alcohol is Okay 
* Luke 7:34
	* Christ may have drunk wine, hence the callout of "drunkard" 
* John 2:1-11 
	* Christ turned water into wine for a party 
* Psalm 104:15 
	* Wine makes man's heart glad 
* Isaiah 25:6 
	* God will prepare a banquet with wine 

# Drunkenness is not Okay 
* Ephesians 5:18 
	* Paul not only calls drunkenness a debauchery, but contrasts it to being filled with the spirit 
	* Paul makes our target to not be controlled by alcohol, but to instead be controlled by the Holy Spirit 
	* To go further, this section (v. 15-21) talks of being imitators of God, _rather_ than walking like the "unwise" (Proverbs 20:1 affirms this)
* Proverbs 20:1 
	* Contrasts being drunk with being wise 
* Isaiah 5:22 
	* Equates something about wine to being wicked? 

# Do not Cause Others to Stumble 
* 1 Corinthians 8 
	* "Paul addresses the question of whether Christians are permitted to eat idol food. This was a significant moral challenge for many early believers living in the Roman world, since various foods offered to idols were afterward eaten in pagan temples (8:10) or sold in the marketplace and eaten at home (10:25-29). Paul insists that the Corinthians must never eat idol food if the danger of idolatry or scandal is in any way present (10:14, 27-29)" (_Ignatius Study Bible_, p. 295)
	* _All_ of chapter 8 talks about knowing that an action isn't a sin, doing that action, and causing a brother to stumble because you gave them the wrong impression of that action 
	* v. 1-6 talk of us having the knowledge of Christ as our only God and Lord 
	* v. 7 talk of those who do not have this knowledge, eating food that has been sacrificed to idols _as if_ it were a true sacrifice to an idol. This causes their conscious to be defiled 
	* v. 8 says that food neither commends us nor causes us to lack; it's just food 
	* v. 9 "but see to it that this authority of yours does not become a stumbling block to the weak" (LSB)
	* v. 10 eating things sacrificed to idols is not a sin to those who are wise in God. But doing so can cause a brother who is not wise in God to stumble, as he does not understand 
	* v. 11-13 Christ died for that brother. Causing him to stumble is a sin against him and also a sin against Christ 
* Romans 14:13-23
	* Do not become a stumbling block to a brother 
	* v. 21 "it is good to not eat meat or to drink wine, or to do anything by which your brother stumbles" (LSB)
	* v. 22 "The faith which you have, have as your own conviction before God. Blessed is he who does not judge himself in what he approves" (LSB)

# Current Convictions
1. 7/3/2024 
	* Alcohol itself is a gift from God to be merry (Psalm 104:15) 
	* Drunkenness is a sin; not simply from the action, but from the absence of control from the Holy Spirit (Ephesians 5:18) 
	* While alcohol is a gift, Christians must not be stumbling blocks to others. Therefore, we must be careful in how we portray alcohol

I wanted to say thanks for the invite to the party, it means a lot! Sadly I will not be there as I'm hanging with my family. I also wanted to send you something in love as someone in Christ, and maybe voice a concern: 
I have personal convictions about how I portray myself to people, as I have the most amazing gift of a relationship with Christ and reflecting His light unto others. The joy I have in God through the Holy Spirit is something that separates me from this world's darkness, and strengthens my love for others and want for sharing the Gospel!
God not only gives us His Spirit for joy, He also gives us joy through His creation. It's a theme that frequents the Psalms (Psalm 8 is great!). This includes nature's beauty (Psalm 145:5), physical refuge (Psalm 46), and alcohol (Psalm 104:15 says wine "gladdens the heart of man"). Even Isaiah 25:6 says God will prepare a feast with "choice wines well refined" - drinking is shown to be a joy given through creation!
My concern is that, as with so many joys, drinking can become a stumbling block. In 1 Corinthians 8 Paul talks of eating food sacrificed to idols: he gives an argument for why it's not a sin, but proceeds to warn against it. He makes clear that, though the action itself is not to be grieved, doing so with a chance of misguiding others is something to be grieved (1 Cor. 8:7, 12-13). Paul also cautions us that being drunk is the opposite of being "filled with the Holy Spirit" (Eph. 5:18), even though Paul isn't against drinking itself (1 Tim. 5:23). 
This is why I felt concern from the end of the party invite. Not that the invite is to be grieved; it's just that inviting minors to watch and others to partake in a drinking game gives me personal concern; from the invite's portrayal of alcohol and from what Paul describes: "it is right not to eat meat or drink wine or do anything that makes your brother stumble" (Rom. 14:21). 
Hopefully this doesn't come across as pious or judgmental - my prayer is that, while we may disagree, you'll understand my concern and that through this we can have a fuller appreciation for the joys God has given us, and how gracious God has been to let us reflect His light!
