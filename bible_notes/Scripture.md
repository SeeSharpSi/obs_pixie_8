# The Only Wise God 

**"Fear God and keep His commandments, because this is the whole duty of man" (Eccl. 12:13b)** 
The purpose of a study Bible is not to equate man's understanding of divine revelation with divine revelation itself. Rather, it's to strengthen christendom by exercising the faculties God gave man and to allow those gifted with teaching to share that gift (1 Cor. 12). Hebrews 2:1-4 exhorts us to "pay closer attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away", directly proceeding a compilation of verses from the Old Testament that show Jesus' glory, deity, kingship, and more. Engaging with scripture's teachings is a primary way to "pay closer attention to what we have heard." 

**"I have discovered that God made men upright, but they have sought out many schemes" (Eccl. 7:29b)** 
The Holy Spirit is perfect, while we are not. A good study Bible will be compiled by people who trust the Holy Spirit's guidance - but they're still people. The notes in a Bible shouldn't be equated with the verses they're attached to. Beyond this, it can be easy to lean into the meaning of a note rather than the meaning of a verse; I definitely struggle with this. Reading scripture knowing what it says _before_ turning to notes is good practice. The notes are meant to elucidate the verses, not define them. Jesus talked of the Old Testament as being God's words, and the writers of the New Testament wrote on Jesus' behalf (Heb. 1:1-2). We are subordinate to God; God has raised Jesus; God has affirmed Jesus' words. We want to "fear God and keep His commandments", not create God's commandments. 

I hope this Bible is a blessing to you!
