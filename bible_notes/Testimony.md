# Write-up 
Let's have a brief prayer before we begin. Bow your head with me: 
	Father, what we know not, teach us.
	What we have not, give us. 
	What we are not, make us.
	For your Son's sake. 
	Amen. 

The Oxford English Dictionary defines "testify" as "To bear witness to, or give proof of (a fact)." 
Tonight I will testify to the fact that God is actively at work in our lives and His creation. 

I was born in Texas, moved to Ohio, moved to California at 9 years old, and then moved to Georgia at 15 years old.
In California, my dad was a pastor at a church with his main focus being worship. It was a good church with a good community, but its attendance started to fade.
Because of fading attendance, the elders started exploring the possibility of merging with a younger, more "hip" church that needed a larger building. 
The team of elders were not honest with the congregation; their end-goal was to appease their local church because they thought the "pros" of merging outweighed the "cons" of honesty. 
My dad would not participate in this, so he eventually left. The ends do not justify the means. 

My vantage-point on these events was blurred by internal conflict, anger, and disillusion towards God and the church. I knew what I believed: that God is good and that all things work towards His good. But I was still buried in uncertainty and inner turmoil. Why would God let a church's leadership abandon their flock and lead them astray? Why would God let me lose my friends? 

Eventually my dad found another pastoral role in Georgia, so we moved. The church we had found seemed good at first, and I quickly made a few new friends. But I always felt alone, and distant from joy and peace. 
Fast-forward one-and-a-half years later, and my dad had realized that the leadership at our new church had internal conflict, shallow views on Christianity, and a broken leadership structure. These things had been purposefully hidden from my dad because their leadership saw the ends as justifying the means. Along with this my dad was treated very, very poorly. 

This local church and its community were the reason we had moved away from our friends and across the country; soon after arriving, we left. 

Why would God let me lose my friends again? 
Why would God let a church hurt my family? 

I was around 17 at this point in time. I still struggled with uncertainty, disillusion, and anger towards God. And it had started to manifest in the view that faith in a higher power was because of a "God in the gaps" mentality; that we only feel like a God exists because we have no better explanation. 

My dad got a job selling furniture, and my family started attending a local church that built into me positively. It allowed me to express the gifts God had given me, and had good men that cared about God. But internally I was still distant from God and had the same mentality; that belief in God is not justifiable. This only exacerbated my turmoil. 

Then I stumbled upon a video of a professor of mathematics at Oxford debating God's existence at the Oxford Union. 
He gave reasons for belief in God. But contained in all of these reasons was a path that ended at Jesus. The professor said in an Irish accent: 
	"The hardest problem I face as a Christian is the problem of evil and pain. My niece getting a tumor at 22 that kills her - what do I say to my sister? And this is the hardest problem we face. 
	But it seems to me that atheism here has no answer. Because by definition atheism believes that human death is the end so there is no _ultimate_ hope.
	But you see: we could stay here 'til midnight and beyond arguing what a good god should might would could if not possibly might just could he not have done and we'll get nowhere.
	So it seems to me there's another question we can ask and it's this: granted that life presents us with a double picture - we see some beautiful things, we see some ragged edges, we see hurt and pain and we see joy. How can we come to terms with that? And it seems to me there is no simple answer but a window into an answer and it's this:
	If it is actually true that Jesus is the Son of God, then we can ask the question "what is God doing on a cross?" 
	And the answer comes back at the very least: God has not remained distant from our human suffering, but has become part of it. 

Jesus died and rose from the dead. I believed this already, but the gravity of this fact, that is wasn't baseless, that it meant everything, that God Himself had entered our suffering, struck me in that moment. 
What also struck me - and continues to strike me - is that God had been good to me all along. In the conflict and turmoil I had loving parents, a home, food. And above all, God was teaching me about His character. 

God is not distant from us.
God is not distant from our suffering. 
God is not the cause of evil. 
God works all things together for good to those who love Him. 

Jesus showed Himself to be true by rising from the dead. 
And He showed His love by dying the death of a wretch. 

My identity is not in giving to the poor. 
or in honesty, 
or in truth, 
or in sharing the Gospel.

My identity is in Christ, as my Lord and Savior. 
Hebrews 1 - 2:2 
Don't neglect the offer of salvation, whether you already believe it, or do not.
