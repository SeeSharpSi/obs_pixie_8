	* "I would argue that those who followed were the elect by God"

## 40-42 (day 1)
* We see "found"
	* If God is to be found by man, he is to first be revealed to man
* we see "son of"
	* "Son of" means the "same nature as"
* divine insight of Jesus
	* v. 42 - Simon's name being changed to Peter (linked to v. 51)
* John wants us to focus on the interaction between Andrew and Jesus
	* After spending the day with Jesus Andrew went to got Simon-Peter, his brother
	* "I would suggest to you Andrew was instructed by Jesus to do this" because 
		* None of these encounters are random
		* did Andrew find Jesus, or did Jesus find Andrew? (v. 37)
		* this was a purposeful predestined act of God; Jesus was finding specifically Andrew and John
* Ephesians 1:11
* we talk about the doctrine of election
	* "I believe it to be a doctrine worthy of your considering, as John puts this doctrine before us in this gospel"
	* "God did not elect everyone; if He had, everyone would come to faith in Christ"
	* "God chose to pursue some, convict them of their sin and lead them to faith"
	* "God has the power and free-will to do this. . . No-one can compel Him to do something against His free-will. God doesn't choose us based on the choices we make, we choose God based on the choice God made"
* v. 42 the focus of this verse is not on Peter, but on Christ. Simon's name was changed by Jesus; what does this tell us about Jesus
	* God changed Abram's name
	* God changed Sarai's name
	* God changed Jacob's name
	* Jesus changed Simon's name
	* Jesus is, therefore, God
	* Isaiah 62:1-4


## 43-51 (day 2)
* v. 51 disciples did not know what this meant at the time
	* John, while writing this, knew very well in hindsight what this mean
* John wrote this gospel around 90AD
	* Christ was crucified around 30AD
	* pre-cross, John thought Jesus was the son of man, being the same nature of man 
	* post-cross, John understands thoroughly that Jesus was the son of man, the son of God, being fully God and man
	* Psalm 2, Jesus is referred to as the Son of God
* God changed Jacob's name to Israel, therefore changing his nature (though oddly, Jacob doesn't usually take on that name)
