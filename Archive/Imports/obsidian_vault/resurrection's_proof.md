* the majority of historian's approaching the new testament as they would any other historical document come to accept the fundamental facts of the resurrection  
	* this isn't specific to conservative or evangelical scholars 
	* this is on the broad spectrum of NT critics 
* these facts are as follows: 
	1. after his crucifixion, Jesus was buried in a tomb of a member of the Jewish Sanhedron known as Joseph of Arimathea. This is important because it means the location of Jesus' tomb was known to both Jew and Christian alike  * NT scholars have established the historicity of this fact with evidence such as: 
		1. Jesus' burial is attested multiply in early independent sources (very important for establishing historical facts) 
		2. Joseph of Arimathea is unlikely to be a Christian invention 
		3. No other competing burial story exists. If the Bible's account of Christ's burial was fabricated, we would expected another story to surface 
	2. On the Sunday following the resurrection, the tomb was found empty by a group of Jesus' followers 
		1. The historicity of the burial account supports the empty tomb. If the burial site of Jesus were known in Jerusalem, it would be impossible for a story of an empty tomb to surface and propagate all of Rome without checking the tomb itself
