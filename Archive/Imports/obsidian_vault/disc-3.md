# breach-1
Facebook encountered a breach before the August of 2019. It infamously chose to to _not_ warn its users that their data had been stolen until nearly two years later. The data included, but was not limited to, names, emails, locations, and phone numbers. 
Facebook never said how they were breached. That said, we do know that they couldn't be sure of which users to notify. This means that it was most likely a socially engineered hack that allowed exploiters to come in direct contact with the data, rather than use a pre-existing pipeline. The impact was presumed to be small, considering much of the data was public facing to begin with. That said, people are capable of a lot with a small amount of information.

# breach-2
In the November of 2019, the Chinese web market company Alibaba was hit with an attack impacting over 1.1 billion user's data. It happened against the company's website Taobao. The attack was said to have lasted over eight months; an exploit allowed people to scrape the website for user information until the company realized what was happening.
The exploit didn't expose anything encrypted. That said, the exploit was severe enough that Alibaba notified police. The full consequences aren't known because happened in China.
Personally, not much may have happened. Private information is scarce in China, considering their government and how it operates. The breach was bad, but could have been worse considering the massive number that was impacted.

Cryptography would have helped Facebook prevent hackers from handling ready-to-read data after it had been acquired. Better personal practices would have helped prevent a socially engineered attack as well.
Alibaba would have greatly benefited from two things: good initial development practices, and a good cyber team to monitory the page. Allowing a site to be manually scrubbed for information is poor design. While allowing that design to last for eight months is poor cyber practice on the side of the developers and the cyber team (if they even had a cyber team).
