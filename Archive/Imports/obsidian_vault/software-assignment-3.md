# RQ 6

## 1
Essentially, the process of garnering software requirements and steps in the engineering process serve one purpose: understanding what functionality is needed by stakeholders. This includes eliciting requirements from them, seeing what technology is feasible, and creating prototypes (technically, requirements engineering doesn't include "how" the software is constructed. But it includes documentation, which means you need an idea of how it will be constructed).

## 3
This six dimensions to keep in mind when gathering requirements are:
* Individual functionality
* Business flow
* Data formats and information needs
* User interfaces
* Systems with other interfaces
* Reliability, performance, security, and adaptability

## 6
The viewpoint-oriented requirement definition method is based on the idea that requirements are in the eye of the beholder, meaning each person views them differently. A good example is Blender vs. Cinema 4D. Blender is an open source, free 3D modeling software that provides a lot of functionality. People are extremely happy with it because it over-provides for its niche; something free doesn't need to provide much, yet Blender does. On the other hand you have Cinema 4D, a paid 3D modeling software. People are also happy with it, but for different reasons. Being paid it can provide customer support, which Cinema 4D does a good job with. It also provides a lot of features. It provides a lot for (and according to) its niche, and people are happy because it meets that niche's expectations.
