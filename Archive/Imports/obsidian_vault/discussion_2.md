a) Confirmed in September of 2019, mobile game developer Zynga was found to be breached for over 170 million usernames and passwords. The breach not only included usernames and passwords, it included email addresses, phone numbers, and Facebook IDs. The size of the breach would make it the 10th largest data breach of all time (at the time of the article).
The main vulnerability exposed was Zynga's use of SHA-1 encryption, which is now considered breakable with "$100,000 of cloud computing." The business impacts weren't huge, unfortunately. They failed to notify users and failed to encrypt their data, resulting in what is considered one of the largest failures of disclosure by cyber experts worldwide. Though customers don't see immediate ramifications, so Zynga continues business as per usual.

b) President of Jumio Robert Prigge suggests that biometric authentication may be necessary to protect against password breach incidents like these in the future. I agree that biometrics provide good protection against attempts to steal data, though frequent password resets and using good encryption may go farther since not every phone supports biometrics. Ideally, apps will have an option for both until all mobile devices contain biometric authentication.

c) The NIST officially retired SHA-1 last year, according to this post, and recommends IT professionals remove it by 2030. It was retired for similar security reasons as Zynga faced; it can be cracked. It was also retired to prevent fraudulent messages in secure programs. According to a computer scientists at NIST, "Modules that still use SHA-1 after 2030 will not be permitted for purchase by the federal government."
