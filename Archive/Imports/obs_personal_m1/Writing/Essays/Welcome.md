# The Internet is Dying 

## Information 
The internet's main purpose is information. Receiving, collecting, showing, aggregating; in whatever capacity, we use the internet to interact with more information than even H. G. Wells would have predicted. And that's extremely powerful. 
Simultaneously, the way people interact with information is special. Isaac Asimov said "You can prove anything you want by coldly logical reason - if you pick the proper postulates." While that may be true to an extent, people are good at detecting falsities - even when lacking good reason - causing the **aggregate** of human reason to often (though not always) be correct. 
This raises an important question: what if a lie _was_ the aggregate of human information? 

## What is truth? 
People have searched for truth for a long time at both the macro and micro. The Bible's oldest extant manuscript is [P52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rylands_Library_Papyrus_P52). It includes John 18:38 - the height of the narrative - which poses the question "What is truth?" 
We base our lives on truth, cognizant or not. Epistemology is a field of academia dedicated to finding what it even means to be true - humans care deeply about truth. Artificial Intelligence won't change the nature of what causes a truth claim to be valid. And it definitely won't change the nature of ultimate, meaningful truth. But it will change the way we interact with information presented to us as being true. We already ask "is this information from someone I can trust," but what happens when we start asking "is this information even from a human"?

## What is AI? 
A Large Language Model (LLM) is a predictive engine that runs off of mass amounts of human generated data. So, essentially, it's an aggregate of the thoughts, styles, and claims engineers feed it. This causes AI to give a general answer - the answer that its data suggests as the most probable - to queries thrown at it. You may ask for the world's population, and if the data given to the LLM includes a probable answer to the world's population, it will respond with that answer.
But this produces important questions: What about the times when the most probable response is incorrect? Or isn't what we want? These questions touch the political, artistic, and historic areas of our lives. 

## The Ability to Create 
Humans have the ability to create things that other humans are attached to. But humans aren't attached to a piece of art or a piece of literature solely for the product; they're attached for the person behind the product. Few people would attend a concert with an AI artist. Fewer people would purchase an expensive painting knowing it was created by AI.

So, what's the difference?

Beauty is beautiful partly because of the connections we have to it's context. For instance: I know that the members of the band Twenty One Pilots are funny, quirky, and relatable, causing their music to be more personable. And I know that Van Gogh's _The Starry Night_ depicts the view through his asylum's window, giving the painting a transcendent hope. And most notably the universe: its beginning via creator gives its spectacle meaning. 

The context matters. And the context of AI is "artificial," which is a negative context. 

## The Internet's Future Context 
An LLM has the ability to quickly produce mass amounts of information. A news article generated by AI is not the opinion of a professional or of a journalist who is on the ground. It's of the average person's view on a subject; and the average person doesn't have the proper vantage-point to answer hard, important questions. They have little to no [ethos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modes_of_persuasion#Ethos). 
Deep down, people know this. They know that Princess Leia's cameo in Rogue One looked "a bit off." They would rather it be the real Carrie Fisher, even _if_ they couldn't tell it was a CGI cameo. Knowing something is false places a type of doubt in the back of someone's mind.

Once a large part of the internet is AI, that doubt will exist in the back of minds whether a piece of content is AI or not, giving all content a negative context.
