# Luggage Buying Guide & Recommendations

*Researched July 2, 2026. Sources: Wirecutter, Travel + Leisure, Consumer Reports.*

---

## What to Look For When Purchasing Luggage

### 1. Size & Type
- **Personal item**: ~18×14×8 in (must fit under seat)
- **Carry-on**: 22×14×9 in (standard for most US airlines; international may be smaller)
- **Checked**: 24–30 in height is common; watch weight limits (usually 50 lb)
- Always check your airline's specific restrictions before buying

### 2. Hard-Sided vs. Soft-Sided
**Hard-Sided (Polycarbonate, ABS, Aluminum)**
- Better protection for fragile items
- Sleeker, more modern look
- Lighter weight options available
- *Downside*: Shows scratches/dents more easily; less flexible for overpacking

**Soft-Sided (Nylon, Polyester)**
- Hides wear better, typically lasts longer
- More flexible — can squeeze in extra items
- Often has exterior pockets for quick access
- *Downside*: Less protection for fragile items; may be heavier

### 3. Wheels
- **4-wheel (Spinner)**: 360° rotation, easier to maneuver in tight spaces, slightly less durable
- **2-wheel (Roller)**: More durable, better on rough surfaces, roll forward/back only
- Look for sealed wheel bearings (prevents dirt/grime from breaking down wheels)

### 4. Handle
- Should extend and retract smoothly with minimal wobble/rattle
- Adjustable height preferred (multiple stops)
- Two-post handles allow piggybacking a second bag
- Handles that retract fully inside the bag are less likely to be damaged

### 5. Zippers
- **YKK zippers** = gold standard for strength and smoothness
- **Chain zippers** (interlocking teeth) = sturdier, harder to break into
- **Coil zippers** (polyester coils) = lighter, more flexible, but less secure

### 6. Weight
- Wheeled bag: aim for **~7 lbs or less** empty
- Non-wheeled: **2–4 lbs** empty
- Remember: the bag's weight counts toward airline limits

### 7. Materials
| Material | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Polycarbonate** | Durable, lightweight, flexible | Can scratch |
| **ABS** | Cheap, lightweight | Less durable than polycarbonate |
| **Aluminum** | Most durable, premium look | Heaviest, expensive, dents show |
| **Ballistic Nylon** | Tough, hides wear, puncture-resistant | Heavier, can abrade over time |
| **Cordura/Nylon** | Softer, abrasion-resistant | Less structured |

### 8. Warranty
- **Lifetime warranty** is ideal — especially one covering airline damage
- Briggs & Riley and Travelpro (Platinum Elite) offer the best warranties
- Check if registration is required (often within 120 days)
- Note: most warranties don't cover cosmetic wear

### 9. Locks
- Must be **TSA-compliant** for US travel (agents can open with master key)
- Built-in TSA locks are convenient; aftermarket locks also work

### 10. Expandability
- Expansion zippers add 1–2 inches of depth
- Great for souvenirs/shopping hauls
- *Warning*: Expanded bags may not fit in overhead bins

### 11. Smart Features
- USB charging ports (bring your own battery pack)
- Built-in luggage tracking (e.g., AirTag holders, July CaseSafe)
- Digital scales, proximity sensors, fingerprint locks
- Note: Lithium-ion batteries **cannot** go in checked bags per FAA rules

### 12. Interior Organization
- Compression straps or panels
- Mesh zippered pockets/dividers
- Garment folders/suiters for formalwear
- Packing cube compatibility
- Shoe bags, laundry bags (some included)

### 13. Other Tips
- **Nesting**: Buy luggage that nests inside itself for easy home storage
- **Test in-store**: Roll it, extend the handle, jiggle the wheels, check interior volume
- **Color**: Darker colors hide scuffs; bright colors are easy to spot at baggage claim
