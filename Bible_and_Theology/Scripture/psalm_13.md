# Psalm 13
* The Psalms record expressions that were prayed and sang 
* The Psalms taken together reflect not only a robust Theology of God, but also a full range of genuine human experience 
* Psalms will often have a Hebrew header to give some context 
* This Psalm has a Hebrew header, translated "To the choirmaster, a Psalm of David" 
* Psalm 34's header is amazing 
	* Psalm 54 
	* Psalm 56 
	* Sometimes Psalms reference a song we no longer have 
	* Psalm 51 
* **1 Corinthians 10:13** 
* v. 1(-2) "how long" 
	* This question involves a statement, showing the psalmist's weariness and burden 
	* **Psalm 94:3, 44:24, 67:1, 31:15-16** 
	* The psalmist here shows sorrow 
* v. 2 Is this Psalm relating to a struggle with an enemy? His kingdom? Maybe David's struggle gives cause for his enemies to rejoice? 
	* v. 3 may indicate sickness or a nearing of death 
* There are many ways we can relate to these feelings and seasons 
* v. 3-4 
	* He's expressing his despondency 
	* There's a difference between what the psalmist feels and what he _knows_ to be bedrock truth 
* v. 5-6 
	* While he doesn't _feel_ God's presence with him, he trusts in God and _knows_ of His goodness 
	* Because of this knowledge, he can do nothing but rejoice in His promise and blessing 
* Imperfect coordinative 
	* "I will sing" vs "I will not sing" 
* This isn't saying you must always smile, or be happy; he's saying that you must have faith 
* Psalm 23 
	* Great promises of the Lord's blessing 
	* In this same Psalm, he expressed "even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil. For You are with me" 
* None of the world's answers will bring us any closer to God 
	* The psalmist is hurting. But he's not hopeless 
	* He knows there's a big difference between what he _feels_ and what he _knows_ 
* 2 Corinthians 10:3-5 
	* We take _every_ thought captive 
* **Psalm 42:11** 
	* Salvation 
* One absolutely critical point: 
	* v. 5 "steadfast love" comes from the Hebrew word "checed" conveys a devotion that's rooted in a conventional relationship 
* Only in Christ can we understand that there's a purpose in sufferings 
* Only in Christ can we join this psalmist in truly declaring our praises to the Lord 
* Three fundamental things the psalmist does in his hurt: 
	1. He prays 
	2. He trusts 
	3. He praises 
* He humbly worships the Lord
