# ch. 15 
* The Lord rejects Saul: 
	* Samuel brings a message from God 
	* God mentions Amalek (v. 2) Ex. 17:8-16  
	* God cursed Amalek in Exodus when they attacked Israel leaving Egypt (Ex. 17:14-16) 
	* v.3 God orders the destruction of every man, women, child, animal 
* [who were the Amalekites?](https://www.gotquestions.org/Amalekites.html) 
	* Mentioned in Gen. 14:7, but they did not yet exist. It's a reference that's "out of its time" 
	* Deuteronomy 25:17-19 recounts the Amalek attack in Ex as having "no fear of God" and says to "not forget" their future, where they're to blot them out. 
	* Saul's failure to blot them out results in hundreds of years of plunder against Israel 
* Saul wants the Kenites to leave (v. 6). Moses's father-in-law was a Kenite and helped establish Israel out of Egypt (Ex. 18) 
* v.9 Saul disobeyed God's command to "devote to destruction" 
	* He let King Agag live 
	* He let the best animals live 
* [Can God have regret?](https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/stewart_don/faq/does-god-know-everything/25-did-the-lord-regret-making-saul-king.cfm) 
	* In v. 11 God says He has regret 
	* God is all-knowing and unchanging (Matt. 10:29-30; Psalm 139:1-4) 
	* God never changes (Psalm 102:27; Hebrews 13:8; Malachi 3:6; Numbers 23:19) 
	* How can we reconcile these with God's regret/repentance/grievance? 
		* 1 Samuel 15:29 - God does not have regret or change His mind 
		* The language used in v. 11 is Anthropopathic
		* Anthropopathic means to "ascribe human feelings to something that is not human" 
		* we find this throughout scripture ([examples](https://www.gotquestions.org/anthropomorphism.html)) 
* v. 13 Samuel immediately called out Saul's lie. In v. 15 Saul lies again 
* v. 21 ". . .but I . . ."
* v. 21b Saul lies again 
* v. 22 obedience is greater than sacrifice (rich man talk to Jesus Matt. 10:21) 
* "Saul needed to see that real worship was indicated by his behavior and not by his sacrifices" 
* v. 24 Saul seems more concerned by consequences than real regret 
* v. 29 "The Glory of Israel" is God
