*Durak is a fast-paced card game that allows everyone to play at once. It has Eastern European origins, and this is our Doukhobor variation.*

![Using Trump to beat cards in Durak](https://berriesandbarnacles.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Using-Trump-to-beat-cards-in-Durak.jpg)

Using Trump to beat cards in Durak

Durak (do-raw-k) is a staple in Brad’s family. A feature of every gathering. Something his mother played, even as her dementia got worse. And it is now a favorite game of our children.

Basically, it is a quick game where the goal is to get rid of all your cards first. The gameplay is simple, but fiercely competitive. Especially, if you are playing with 3+ players, and even more so if you play with “double-backs”. Think of [Uno](https://amzn.to/4qwWcqq), but with the ability to gang up on each other.

While ganging up seems fierce and stressful, the game is so quick (less than 15 minutes a hand) that it is usually a friendly competition.

## Game Set Up

A single deck of cards works for up to 5 players. However, you can add a second deck for larger groups. I don’t recommend playing with more than 8 people, as it becomes difficult to see each other’s cards, which is needed for Durak. If you’re looking for games for more than 8 players, I recommend [Privilege](https://berriesandbarnacles.com/how-to-play-president-a-card-game-about-privilege/) or [Oh Hell](https://berriesandbarnacles.com/how-to-play-oh-hell-a-game-for-3-to-10-players/).

Each player is dealt 6 cards, and they maintain a hand of 6 cards by drawing from the draw pile as the game progresses.

The trump suit is determined by flipping over the top card from the draw pile after all the hands have been dealt. The flipped-over card can later be exchanged for the 2 of trump. A brag-worthy bonus if the visible trump is a face card and you happen to be holding the 2.

## Durak Game Play

1. The person to the left of the dealer is the Attacker. They get to play their cards first. They can play a single, double, or multiple cards as long as the cards are all the same number (different suits). The goal is to get rid of all your cards first, so typically people lay down their lowest cards.
2. The person to the left of the Attacker is the Defender. They have to beat each of the cards that the Attacker played with a higher-value card of the same suit. The Defender can also choose to use a trump suit card to beat one of the cards.
	- This may be necessary if they don’t have higher cards in a particular suit.
		- It may also be desirable to prevent breaking up pairs.
3. Things get more complicated because everyone else in the game can also play cards if they are the same number as a card already laid down by the Attacker or the Defender. This is limited by the number of cards in the Defender’s hand. Or if the Defender has picked up a lot of cards, it’s limited to a maximum of 6 cards.
	- This creates a race for the other players to get in on the action, adding speed to the gameplay.
![Trumping in an attack in a game of durak](https://berriesandbarnacles.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Trumping-in-an-attack-in-a-game-of-durak.jpg)

In this round, ♣️ is the trump suit, as shown by the 4 next to the draw pile. The Attacker played two 3s, and the Defender used a trump to beat one of them.

### If a Defender can’t beat an attack

4. If the Defender is unable to beat all of the cards played, they must pick up all the cards on the table. Generally, this results in picking up a bunch of low-value cards.
5. The Defender also loses their turn to attack. The next Attacker will be the person to their left. They can, however, play cards during other players’ turns.
6. If you don’t think you’ll be able to defend against an attack, it’s better to pick up the cards right away. Once the Defender starts beating cards, they are at risk of more attacks because players can lay down as many as 6 cards as long as they are the same value as those already played. For example, if the Attacker plays a 3 ♠️ and the defender beats it with a Queen ♠️, all the other players can play a 3 or a Queen for the Defender to beat.
![Using Trump to beat cards in Durak](https://berriesandbarnacles.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Using-Trump-to-beat-cards-in-Durak.jpg)

After beating the two 3s, another player played a 5 ♦️ based on the 5 ♣️ that the Defenders used to beat one of the 3s. Now the defender must also beat the 5 ♦️.

### At the end of the attack

7. The attack ends either when the Defender picks up all the cards or beats all the cards in an attack (a maximum of six extra cards).
8. Everyone must draw cards until their hands hold 6 cards. If someone has more than 6 cards (because they were unable to beat an earlier attack), then they don’t draw any extra cards.
	- The order of draw is: the Attacker first, then all other players who participated in the attack. The Defender draws last.
9. Once cards have been beaten, they are set aside in a discard pile.
10. If the Defender beats all the cards, it is their turn to attack the person to their left.
11. The game continues until all of the cards have been played.
	- Once the draw pile is finished, the players must try to get rid of all the remaining cards in their hands.
		- In Doukhobor Durak, there is no winner. The loser is the last person still holding cards. Durak means *fool* or *crazy*, and the loser is the fool. 😉

## Playing with Double-Backs

Playing with double-backs is where Durak becomes really fast and competitive. If you are new to the game, I recommend playing the simple way first, at least until you get used to the mechanics of the game.

Double-backs require just one additional rule:

1. If the Defender holds a card that is the same number as the card played by the Attacker, they can lay it down and force the Attacker to beat the cards instead.
	- This only works for the first card(s) played. Once the Defender has beaten a card, they can no longer double-back.
		- For example, if the Attacker plays a 6 ♠️, the Defender may play a 6 ♦️. Then the roles of Attacker and Defender are swapped.
		- The direction of gameplay also switches, so the next turn will involve the original Attacker playing cards for the person on their right (provided the Attacker managed to beat both of the played 6s).
		- Reverse double-backs are possible! If the Attacker plays a 6 ♠️, the Defender may play a 6 ♦️ to switch the attack. But it can be countered by a 6 ♣️. This results in the original Defender having to beat all three 6s. 😅

Double-backs certainly add a lot of fast and furious fun to a game of Durak!
