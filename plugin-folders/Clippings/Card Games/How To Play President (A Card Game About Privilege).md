*Learn how to play President, a card game that clearly demonstrates the power of privilege. It’s an easy and fun game for large groups.*

![[Pairs-being-beaten-by-a-single-two-of-hearts.jpg|President is a card game that demonstrates the power of privilege]]

Twos are high, and a single 2 can beat a pair.

President is a card game known by lots of other names… most of them will get me flagged for rudeness, so I won’t list them off here! 😉 However, we like to call this game Privilege, and I hope the name sticks because it’s the perfect way to gain an understanding of what Privilege looks like.

This is a game we play a LOT. It’s perfect for large groups. People who don’t normally play cards can easily learn this very simple game. It’s great for groups of children and adults. And it is a leisurely game played over conversations and snacks.

During the summer, we head over to our [shabin](https://berriesandbarnacles.com/category/shabin/) most weekends, often with a bunch of extra people coming along, and Privilege is our go-to evening activity!

## The Basics

Privilege is a shedding card game for 3 + players. Though it’s much more fun with 5+ players. This means the goal of the game is to get rid of all your cards first.

Here are some of the key features.

- Twos are high. Then it runs Aces, Kings, Queens, all the way down, with Threes being the lowest cards.
- Jokers are wild.
- After the first round, the President gives cards to the Loser, and the Loser gives their best cards to the President. This is why we call it Privilege. Because once you start to win, it requires quite a bit of bad luck to fail.
- If you are playing with a large group, you can make several layers of hierarchy (Vice President and Vice Loser). With a really large group, we also include a Vice-Vice President and Vice-Vice Loser.
- There are two optional “house rules” that help to even things out a bit.
	1. The Loser is the only one who can deal or clear the cards at the end of a round. If someone else clears the cards, they automatically become the new Loser.
		2. The seating order shuffles around the President, so the Loser sits on the President’s left and automatically gets an advantage of playing first on the President’s low cards.

## How To Play

### 1\. Dealing

- Privilege is played with 3+ players and uses a regular, [52-card deck](https://amzn.to/4ggMbIa) with the option of using two decks if there are more than 6 players.
- After the first round, the Loser is the dealer and sweeper (the person who clears the cards at the end of the round).
- The entire deck, including 2 jokers, is dealt, even if it’s not dealt evenly. Having extra cards isn’t necessarily a disadvantage in this game.

### 2\. Game Play

The goal is to be the first to get rid of all your cards. And there are consequences to coming in last.

- In the first round, the person to the left of the dealer gets to start. For subsequent rounds, the President gets to start. Starting has a huge advantage because it gives the player a chance to get rid of their lowest cards.
- Every card laid must be higher than the card laid before it. If a single card is led, then all other cards played in the round must be singles. This follows for pairs, triples, etc. If you’re playing with 2 decks, you can build up some powerful multiples.
- In this game, Threes are low, and Aces are high. Twos are a special card because one less Two is needed to beat Aces. For example, 2 Twos can beat 3 Aces. Jokers are wild, and a single Joker can beat any combination of cards. (See photo above for an example round).
- Players aren’t required to play in a round. Sometimes it’s an advantage to save your high cards for later on in the game. Particularly if you’re worried about someone using a Joker to beat you. In that case, the player says “pass” when it’s their turn to play.
- However, once someone has passed playing in a round, they can’t re-enter the game until the next round. No last-minute jumping in!
- The round ends when no one is able (or willing) to play another card. Then the person who played the last card gets to start the next round. This is an advantage because it allows them to get rid of their lowest cards.
- The rounds continue until there is only one player left in the game.

### 3\. Scoring

There is no official score or scorekeeping. This is why it’s perfect for camping or ferry rides!

- The winner is the first person to play all their cards. They become the President.
- The person still holding cards when everyone else has gone out is the Loser.
- Depending on how many players there are, you may want to keep track of the order of all the players so you can create several levels of Presidents and Losers.
- For the next round, the Loser must give their best card (or cards) to the President. And the President must give the Loser a card (or cards) as well. But they can choose whatever cards they want.
	- If you’re playing with 3 to 5 people, the Loser would give 1 card, and the President would give 1 card.
		- If you’re playing with 5+ players, you can have VPs exchanging cards as well. In that case, the Loser gives the President their top 2 cards, and the President gives 2 cards to the Loser. The VP and VL exchange 1 card each.
		- This can expand to include 3 layers… so the Loser gives away 3 cards, with the VL giving away 2 cards and the VVL giving away 1 card. You could probably do more layers of hierarchy… but then the Privilege would be VERY entrenched and probably no one would want to play. 😉
- There are two optional rules that help mix things up in the game, preventing someone from getting stuck in the Loser position for more than a few hands. I described them in The Basics section above.

Enjoy!
