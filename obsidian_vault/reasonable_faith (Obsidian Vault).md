## Historical Background 
* Augustine (354-430) 
	* his views evolved over the years, so they can be hard to interpret 
	* 29.3 sometimes seems like a strict authoritarian (held that scripture has the prime, most divine source of authority) 
		* scripture is completely void of error 
		* scripture is to be believed absolutely 
	* such a belief seems to imply that reason has no role in the justification of belief 
		* read the septuagint's version of Is. 7:9 - "to believe, you must first understand"
		* he is seen as often teaching that faith must be held to understand, and/or that faith is required before knowledge 
	* still, he often showed that he was not an unqualified authoritarian
		* he said that faith and reason cooperate in bringing someone do belief  
		* while belief in scripture would lend towards it being the ultimate authority, people have to arrive at that view first before exploring its authoritative assertions 
	* big question: why accept the authority of the past? 
		* Augustine did not have the historical knowledge present to us today. So instead of relying on that for his belief, he relied on the "miracle" of God's earthly church 
		* this doesn't mean he holds the Church's authority over scripture. Rather, the church's existence makes evident the truth of scripture, which in turn causes him to believe in its authority 
		* this is actually not authoritarian at all; rather, it's based on a present miracle 
		* but Augustine promoted the authority of scripture to enough of an extent to set the tone for subsequent medieval theology 
* Thomas Aquinas 32.0 
	* _Summa contra gentiles_
		* showed there are truths that surpass our human understanding (i.e. the trinity) 
		* still, many truths lie within the grasp of human reason (i.e. the existence of God) 
	* In volumes 1-3, he tackles human-graspable topics like the existence of God 
	* In the fourth volume, he starts to tackle ungraspable things like the trinity, sacraments, the incarnation, etc. 
	* This makes Aquinas seem authoritative, and outside of reason 
		* he's not, and carefully treads the issue 
		* he makes clear that these things are not empirically evident. Though he makes no suggestion that truths of faith surpass Aristotelian logic 
		* Example: God can be proved from His effects, while the trinity relies solely on logic and reason 
	* Believed that we should only use arguments that prove with certainty, rather than probability. A case for probability will only reaffirm non-believers in their unbelief 
	* Aquinas's procedure can be summarized in three steps: 
		1. Fulfilled prophecies and miracles make it credible that the Scriptures taken together as a whole are a revelation from God
		2. As a revelation from God, Scripture is absolutely authoritative
		3. Therefore, those doctrines taught by Scripture that are neither demonstrably provable nor empirically evident may be accepted by faith on the authority of Scripture 
	* Thus, Aquinas can say that an opponent may be convinced of the truths of faith on the basis of the authority of Scripture as confirmed by God with miracles/prophecy 
	* This raises the question: how do we know that the purported miracles or fulfilled prophecy ever took place? 
		* Medieval thinkers, lacking the historical method, could not answer this question 
		* About the only argument was Augustine's indirect proof from the miracle of the church 

## The Enlightenment 
* Known as the Age of Reason. Still, the relationship between faith and reason was not completely agreed upon 
* John Locke, 1632-1704 (34.2) 
	* believed that religious belief required a foundation, and attempted to provide one 
	* gave a cosmological argument for the existence of God, and believed that existence of God is "the most obvious truth that reason discovers," having evidence "equal to mathematical certainty" 
	* when one moves beyond matters of demonstrable reason into matters of faith, Locke insisted that ==revealed truths cannot contradict reason== as well as truths unattainable by reason 
	* reason gives greater certainty of these than revelation 
	* truths of faith cannot contradict truths of reason, because truths of reason will always be more certain than purported revelations of faith that contradict reason 
	* no proposition contrary to reason can be accepted as divine revelation 
	* while we know a revelation from God is absolutely true, it lies within the scope of reason to determine if a supposed revelation really is from God to determine its meaning 
	* 34.5 
* Henry Dodwell, 1700-1784 (35.4) 
	* Argues that faith is a work of the Holy Spirit, not of rationality 
	* a true belief in God cannot be led by reason; rather, God saves those who His spirit has worked on. Those believers arrive to faith because of the Holy Spirit and not by reason 

## Contemporary 
* Karl Barth, 1886-1968 & Rudolf Bultmann, 1884-1976 (36.2) 
	* Karl, dialectical 
	* Rudolf, existential 
	* both characterized by authoritarianism 
	* Barth believed that there can be no approach to God via human reason 
		* seems to stem from his thought that God is "wholly other" and transcends all categories of human thought and logic 
		* led him to deny the Roman Catholic doctrine of an analogy being between God and man 
			* this doctrine reads that creation of the product of its Creator, and therefor shares in an analogous way certain properties possessed most perfectly by God such as being, goodness, truth, and so forth 
		* according to Barth, God is so transcendent that no analogy exists between him and the creature (though, the issue is that God is powerful enough to purposefully create this analogy) 
		* this may seem like fideism is the only way to christ. This is not precisely correct 
		* Barth believed that, lost in sin, man cannot even begin to _move_ in the direction of faith, so even a leap in faith is impossible without God moving first 
		* "knowledge of God is a knowledge completely effected and determined from the side of its object, from the side of God" 
		* "_the fact that_ he did come to this decision, _that_ he really believed, and that he actually had freedom to enter this new life of obedience and hope - all this was not he work of _his_ spirit, but the work of the _Holy_ Spirit" 
	* like Barth, Bultmann also rejects any human apprehension of the Word of God (which he seems to identify primarily with the call to authentic existence embodied in the gospel) apart from faith 
		* he considers it essential to faith that it involves risk and uncertainty 
		* therefore, rational evidence is not only irrelevant, but contrary to faith 
		* faith, in order to be faith, must exist in an evidential vacuum 
		* this seems to be fideistic, but that is not quite correct 
			* Bultmann insists that the very authority of God strips away all demands for criteria 
		* his final appeal is authoritarianism

## role of the Holy Spirit 
* 43.5 Plantinga and Dodwell seem correct in that, fundamentally, the way we know Christianity is true is by the self-authenticating witness of God's Holy Spirit 
	* The experience of the Holy Spirit is veridical and unmistakable (though not necessarily irresistible or indubitable) 
* How does the Holy Spirit affect believers vs unbelievers? 

### _The Believer_ 
* New believers receive the Holy Spirit 
	* Gal. 3:26, 4:6 
	* Romans 8:15-16 
* Paul uses the term _plerophoria_ to indicate that a believer has knowledge of the truth as a result of the Spirit's work 
	* Col. 2:2; 1 These. 1:5; cf. Rom. 4:21; 14:5; Col. 4:12 
* The apostle John also makes it clear that the Holy Spirit gives believers conviction of the truth of Christianity 
	* 1 John 2:20, 27 
	* John 14:26 
* 1 John 4:1-3 is not talking of the Holy Spirit itself, but of testing others that say their claim comes from the Holy Spirit 
	* John speaks of this earlier in 1 John 2:18-19 
* The inner witness of the Holy Spirit isn't to be tested. Rather, the claims that others attribute to the Holy Spirit should be tested since they're external 
* The Holy Spirit gives the believer certainty that Jesus lives with him 
	* John 14:16-17, 20 
* 46.2 "the water" is probably baptism, "the blood" His crucifixion 
	* These are the two events that marked the beginning and end of his earthly ministry 
* 46.3 while arguments and evidence may be used to support the believer's faith, they are never properly the basis of that faith 

### _The Unbeliever_ 
* 46.4 Does the absence of the Holy Spirit in the unbeliever mean they just rely on arguments and evidence to be convinced Christianity is true? 
	* Not at all 
	* God has a special ministry for the Holy Spirit for the unbeliever (John 16:7-11) 
* 47.5 "Therefore, we find that for believer and unbeliever alike it is the self-authenticating work of the Holy Spirit that supplies knowledge of Christianity's truth. Thus, I would agree that belief in the God of the Bible is a properly basic belief and emphasizes that it is the ministry of the Holy spirit that supplies the circumstances for its proper basicality" 

## Role of Argument and Evidence 
* _Magisterial_ vs _Ministerial_ use of reason (coined my Martin Luther) 
	* The magisterial use of reason occurs when reason stands above the gospel, as though it judges it based on argument and evidence 
	* The ministerial use of reason occurs when reason submits to and serves the gospel 
	* In light of the Sprit's witness, only the ministerial use of reason is legitimate 
* The christian who sees legitimacy for their faith in evidence has two sources of warrant for their belief, which can give them more confidence in the veracity of scripture, Jesus, and God 

### _A Danger_
* Some say that we shouldn't defend the faith. Rather, we should preach the gospel and let the Holy Spirit do the work 
	* This attitude is unbalanced and unscriptural 
	* As long as reason is a minister to the christian faith, we should employ it 

### _An Objection_ 
* Some christians say that those already saved should employ the magisterial outlook when reasoning on the Bible 
	* Other religions claim divine revelation as well, so we must combat it 
* But why would their claims undermine _my_ knowing the truth of christianity via the Spirit's witness? 
* If a Mormon or Muslim falsely claims to have experienced the witness of God's spirit in their hearts, that does nothing to undermine the veridicality of the Spirit's witness to me 
* The magisterial outlook is also irrational 
	* The vast majority of the human race would have the time, training, nor resources to develop a full-blown Christian apologetic as the basis for their faith 
	* Even proponents for this view at one time in the course of their education lacked such an apologetic
