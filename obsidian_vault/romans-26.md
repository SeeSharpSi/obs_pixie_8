# Preliminary 
Acts 25 closes with Festus introducing King Agrippa to his current situation with Paul. Agrippa asks to hear Paul's case (Acts 25:22). A few points:
* Festus wants something to write to Caesar (Acts 25:26-27)
* Paul will be examined by Agrippa and the "prominent men of the city" (Acts 25:23)

# Herod Agrippa II
Agrippa was the last of the Herods who played a prominent role in NT history 
* His father killed James (Acts 12:1-2) and imprisoned Peter
* His great uncle was the Herod of the Gospels 
* His great grandfather was Herod at Jesus' birth

# The Apostle Paul 
This chapter comes consequent of Paul's appeal to Caesar
* Paul's main purpose wasn't his defense, it was preaching the gospel
* Paul knows his calling was Rome (Acts 23:11)
* God was to use Paul as a "chosen instrument" for spreading the gospel to the gentiles 
	* Acts 9:15 
	* Acts 18:6 
	* Acts 22:21

# Romans 26
This chapter comes consequent of Festus bringing Paul's case to King Agrippa, saying he needs something to write to Rome (Acts 25:26-27) and King Agrippa wanting to hear Paul himself

## v. 1-3
* Agrippa is familiar with the customs and controversies of the Jews

## v. 4-11
Paul gives his Jewish background, namely him persecuting the church
* The Jews were familiar with Paul's life as Saul (v. 4)
* Paul calls out the Jews' misunderstanding of the OT and who their faith is in (v. 7)
* The chief priests gave Paul authority to persecute the church (v. 10)
* Paul wanted Christians to renounce their faith in Christ (v. 11)
<div class="pb"></div> 

# v. 8
Paul found it inconceivable that he should be condemned for believing in the resurrection - the great hope of the Jewish people (Acts 24:15)
* Job 19:25-27
	* Job talking to his friends, affirming his faith in his redeemer (God/Jesus)
* Daniel 12:2
* Psalm 49:15
* Psalm 71:20
* Ecclesiastes 3:21 

Paul stood on traditional, biblical Jewish beliefs. The Sadducees did not and vocally refuted them.

# Paul is the traditionalist, not the Sadducees
Paul believed in Christ not only because of his experience on the road to Damascus, but because the OT prophesied of Him. In contrast, Sadducees do not believe in the resurrection of the dead; this is in friction with much of the OT's prophesies
* Genesis 49:10
* Deuteronomy 18:15 - Acts 3:22 confirms this as Jesus, and the Jews agreed that it was a savior-like messiah 
	* Deuteronomy 34:10
	* Matthew 11:11 

Paul felt great remorse for the Jews. He understood that they were their own destruction and ignorant to God's calling and message. This would result in their spiritual and physical destruction
* Romans 9:1-8
* Amos 9:9-10 (see Acts 15:16-18)

## v. 12-18
* Paul talks of his conversion
* In Paul's testimony, God's voice was "not understood" by those around Paul. This isn't the first time God's voice has been heard but not understood (v. 14)
	* John 12:28-29
* A goad is a short spear used to direct livestock. Kicking against God will not impede on His will; His intended direction will happen
<div class="pb"></div>

## v. 19-23
* Paul talks of the gospel. Like usual, Paul's preaching heavily points towards the OT; Paul knew the messiah's sufferings were taught in the OT
	* Isa. 50:6
	* Isa. 53:3
	* Matthew 12:38-40
	* Hebrews 11:17-19 & Romans 4:25
* v. 21 happened in Acts 21:27-29
* Paul says he would like to convert Agrippa and all around him (v. 27-29)
> [!bible]+ [Acts 26:22 - ESV](https://bolls.life/ESV/44/26/)
> 22. To this day I have had the help that comes from God, and so I stand here testifying both to small and great, saying nothing but what the prophets and Moses said would come to pass:

## v. 24-29
Festus calls Paul mad with a "loud voice," but Paul remains humble and bold, telling Festus what he speaks is true and rational. The question Paul asks in verse 27 proves that he is of right mind. Agrippa is pinned between two choices: 
* Affirm his belief in the prophets, making himself look crazy to the romans or
* Declare the prophets false, angering the Jews

Agrippa parries Paul's question with his own, asking if Paul can "persuade me in a short time to be a Christian" (v. 28). Paul responds famously in verse 29:
> [!bible]+ [Acts 26:29 - ESV](https://bolls.life/ESV/44/26/)
> 29. And Paul said, “Whether short or long, I would to God that not only you but also all who hear me this day might become such as I am — except for these chains.”

This furthers our understanding (and Agrippa's) of Paul's goal: to preach the gospel to those around him.

## v. 30-32 
> [!bible]+ [Acts 26:32 - ESV](https://bolls.life/ESV/44/26/)
> 32. And Agrippa said to Festus, “This man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar.”

Paul appealed to Caesar under his call to preach to the gentiles and to Rome. We cannot be sure if he knew he was to be set free apart from this appeal. But we do know that Paul would have appealed regardless, and that his calling was not only to preach to the gentiles, but to preach in Rome.

# Takeaways/Applications
* Paul preached the gospel boldly, regardless of his circumstance
* Paul was humble, no matter the circumstance

It is easy to cave to the world. People resort to being mild about the Bible; some even blatantly ignore it, with 1 Timothy 3 being a prime candidate. Paul's journey should be convicting to us. The Jews put him on the defense, taking him to court over his "disregard" for the temple (Acts 21:27-29). Paul defends himself with a large emphasis on gospel. He does not shy away from God's word.
Likewise, we should strive for a boldness in and for Christ. A boldness that manifests in humility and unwavering, without shrinking from spiritual challenge. Kicking against the goads will not change God's perfect, good, holy will.
